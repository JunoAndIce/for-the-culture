## Background artwork

- `design/background-1.jpg` (3000×2000) → `public/background-1.webp` — the
  "Black History Month" lettering painted out, then re-encoded (710KB JPEG to
  180KB WebP). Only pixels brighter than the flat charcoal background were
  repainted, so the darker brush texture behind the lettering survived.
- `design/background-2.jpg` (1920×1280) → `public/background-2.webp` — no
  lettering to remove; re-encoded only (627KB to 193KB).
- `design/background-3.jpg` (3000×2000) → `public/background-3.webp` — the
  illustrated figure and the headline/lorem text removed, then re-encoded
  (320KB to 252KB). The figure spanned the full height across both patterned
  borders, so the erased areas were rebuilt by resampling each band at a whole
  multiple of its own repeat (borders ~217px, zigzag field 69.6px) rather than
  filled flat. The canvas was then widened to 5000×2000 by the same periodic
  resampling: at 3:2 an `object-cover` backdrop crops vertically on any
  landscape viewport, which ate the top and bottom borders. 2.5:1 makes cover
  scale to height instead, so the borders survive every viewport up to that
  aspect. The source's last ~15 columns are edge-clamped and non-periodic, so
  it is truncated to 2909 before tiling.

Each wallpaper also has a light-theme twin, `*-light.webp`, generated by
applying a 3D LUT with ffmpeg:

- Near-neutral pixels (the dark field and its brushwork) have their lightness
  inverted, so the field goes near-white and the relationships hold.
- Saturated pixels keep hue and chroma untouched — a wholesale invert would
  send the red to cyan.
- The two branches are blended by chroma rather than switched at a threshold,
  so anti-aliased edges do not band.
- Yellow is capped at L 0.62. At its original L 0.826 it is lighter than the
  light field and would vanish.
